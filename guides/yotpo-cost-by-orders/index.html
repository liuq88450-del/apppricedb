Yotpo Reviews cost by order volume: the 1,001-order cliff

Yotpo prices reviews on monthly order volume. The price table is not on the page as a table; it is embedded in the page as data and drawn by a slider. We rendered the page and read every band, so you can see what a single order over a boundary actually costs.

All 12 published bands below were read from the rendered yotpo.com/pricing page on 2026-11-27, from the pricing data the page itself ships. The Starter band 501–1,000 is marked $129; the band above it is marked disabled, which is what forces the jump. Premium and Enterprise are custom quotes and we do not guess them. The bands below are monthly-billing prices; annual billing exists and saves 20% on Starter and 25% on Pro.

The two plans that carry published prices

Two things the pricing-page slider does not show you: Yotpo also has a free plan (the App Store listing says "Free plan available"), and it offers annual billing that saves 20% on Starter and 25% on Pro (the App Store listing quotes the discount by region, so it does not display one fixed USD figure). The 1,000-order boundary, correctly stated

  • At 1,000 orders the cheapest published plan is Starter at $129.
  • At 1,001 orders the pricing slider marks Starter as unavailable. The next published plan is Pro at $299 — a $170 step, 132% more, or $2,040 a year if it lasts all year.

But that is not the only way out, and most articles miss this. Yotpo runs an option called Reviews Booster on both Starter and Pro. Its own documentation describes it plainly:

If you enable Reviews Booster and then exceed your monthly order limit, we'll charge your monthly subscription fee plus the number of additional orders you used. … You can cap your additional orders to make sure you stay in control of your spending, and you won't be charged for any additional orders you don't use.

So a store at 1,001 orders has three routes, not one: stay under the line, enable Booster and pay per extra order, or move up to Pro. The per-order rate for Booster is not published on the pricing page — the documentation says it "will vary according to your Yotpo plan" — so the $170 step is the cost of upgrading, not a charge that is forced on you.

One trap worth knowing: the same documentation warns that extra orders only cover requests made after you enable Booster. Orders that already passed the limit before you turned it on do not get request emails.

For scale: 1,001 orders and 2,000 orders both cost $299 on Pro. So a store that just crossed the line pays the same as one doing nearly double the volume — unless it uses Booster instead.

Why the table is not on the page

Yotpo shows one price at a time through a volume slider, and the bands exist only as data behind it. That is why most articles quote "from $15" — true for a store with 50 orders, meaningless for anyone deciding whether they can afford reviews at 1,500 orders. We publish the bands because the decision is made at a boundary, not at the entry price.

How to read your own number

  1. Find your monthly order count, not your yearly one. Yotpo charges on the monthly figure.
  2. Locate the band. Up to 1,000 orders Starter is available; above that the slider marks it unavailable, so your choice is Booster (pay per extra order, cap set by you) or Pro.
  3. If you are within about 50 orders of 1,000 and your volume is seasonal, keeping a month under the line can avoid the $170 step — but this only works if the cycle that bills you is the quiet month, because Yotpo charges on the order volume in the billing period. And check whether the review features you actually use exist on Starter at all: this page compares prices, not feature sets.
