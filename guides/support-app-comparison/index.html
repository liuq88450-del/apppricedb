Gorgias vs Tidio vs Help Scout: the same support volume, three pricing models

These three are not priced on the same unit, so the comparison most articles make — entry price against entry price — is meaningless. Gorgias bills per ticket with overage, Tidio bills per conversation quota, Help Scout bills per seat regardless of volume. Here is what each costs at the volume you actually have.

All figures read from the vendors' own pricing pages on 2026-11-27: Gorgias Starter $10 (50 tickets, $0.40 overage), Basic $60 (300, $0.40), Pro $360 (2,000, $0.36); Tidio human-only quota $29 at 100 conversations, $69 at 300, $109 at 500, $199 at 1,000, quote above that; Help Scout Standard $25 per seat per month. We assume 2 agents on Help Scout and monthly billing everywhere. Gorgias AI interactions cost extra ($0.90–$1.00 each) and also count against ticket volume.

The three models, plainly

  • Gorgias charges for volume. $10–$360 for the plan plus overage, so a busy month is a bigger bill. AI replies cost $0.90–$1.00 each on top and also consume ticket volume — they count twice.
  • Tidio charges for a quota of conversations. A conversation is one unit however long it runs, and it only counts once someone replies. Above 1,000 conversations a month the page stops publishing a price.
  • Help Scout charges for people, not tickets. Two agents on Standard is $50 whether you handle 100 tickets or 2,000. That is why it looks expensive at low volume and unbeatable at high volume with a small team.

What that means for the decision

The last column is the useful one. At 1,000 tickets a month Gorgias costs about $340, which is the same as Help Scout with about 13.6 Standard seats. So:

  • Small team, high volume: Help Scout wins on price, by a lot. A three-agent store doing 1,000 tickets pays $75 there against $340 on Gorgias.
  • Big team, low volume: the seat model gets expensive fast — ten agents on Help Scout is $250 before add-ons (extra inboxes $10 each, extra docs sites $20 each).
  • Chat-first rather than ticket-first: Tidio's quota is the cheapest of the three up to about 300 conversations and stays competitive to 1,000, but its ceiling is the 1,000-conversation quota.
