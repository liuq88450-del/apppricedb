All-in-one app vs four single-purpose apps: which costs less

A bundle looks cheaper per feature and is not a replacement for deep tools. The honest comparison is total bill against total capability, at your order volume.

Prices checked 2026-11-13: Vitals $29.99 (up to 200 orders) / $79 (201–1,000); Klaviyo Email from $20, Judge.me $15, Gorgias Starter $10, Weglot Starter $17. Entry tiers only — every line in the right column rises with volume or contacts.

The arithmetic

  • At under 200 orders a month, Vitals at $29.99 is less than half the $62 of four single-purpose entry plans. That is a real saving and the reason bundles sell.
  • At 201–1,000 orders the two lines are $79 against $62 — the bundle is now more expensive than the four apps, because the single-purpose entry tiers have not moved yet.
  • The bundle covers many jobs shallowly; email deliverability, helpdesk automation and translation quality are where single-purpose tools earn their extra cost. If a store leans on any one of those, the bundle is the expensive choice even when the invoice is smaller.
  • Compare like for like: price the bundle against the sum of the apps it actually replaces, not against one app, and price the single-purpose stack at the tiers your real volume puts you in.

What the bundle invoice does not show

  • Overlap: a store running Vitals may still buy a dedicated review app for photo reviews, so the "40+ apps" number overstates what replaces a subscription.
  • Exit cost: consolidating later means moving data out of five systems instead of one.
  • Growth: the bundle moves bands by orders, the stack moves by order, contacts, tickets and words — which one gets expensive first depends on where the store grows.
