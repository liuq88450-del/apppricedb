Weglot cost by number of words, and why the word count never renews

Weglot's plans are not monthly allowances. Each plan includes a total number of unique words, and that limit is fixed for the life of the plan: it does not reset next month. That single fact changes how you should size the plan.

All six tiers were read from weglot.com/pricing on 2026-11-27, in US dollars (the page also quotes euros). The word limits are the vendor's own wording: "Total unique words translated across all your languages. This is a fixed limit and does not renew."

The trap in a lifetime word count

Every other tier is priced against a monthly meter. Weglot's meter never resets, which means the plan you buy is really a ceiling on how much site you can ever translate before paying again:

  • Starter (10,000 words, $17) suits a brochure site that will not change. A store that adds 5,000 words a year exhausts it in two years.
  • Business (50,000 words, $32) is the real entry point for a working catalogue, because product pages and collection pages both count.
  • The price per 1,000 words falls hard: $1.70 on Starter, $0.64 on Business, $0.44 on Pro, $0.33 on Advanced, $0.15 on Extended. Buying a tier above your need is often cheaper per word than upgrading later, but only if you actually use the words.
  • Languages are bundled, not free: 1 on Starter, 3 on Business, 5 on Pro, 10 on Advanced, 20 on Extended. If you need five languages, you cannot stay on Business however few words you have.

How to size it

Count the unique words on the pages you intend to translate, add what you expect to publish over the next two years, then pick the first tier that covers that number and the language count you need. Because the limit does not renew, undersizing costs a full plan upgrade, not a top-up.
