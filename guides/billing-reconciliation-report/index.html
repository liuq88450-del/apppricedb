Shopify app bill reconciliation: the report we run on a real invoice

An invoice tells you what you paid. A reconciliation report tells you what the published plan says you should have paid, line by line, and where the two disagree.

Template built from the billing rules in our rules library, checked 2026-11-06. The worked example uses a fictional store: every "expected" figure is computed from vendor prices read on the dates shown on each app page, and every invoice figure is illustrative. No real invoice is reproduced here.

What the report contains

  1. Store profile — the meters that set the tiers: orders per month, tickets per month, support seats, marketing contacts, SMS messages, translated words.
  2. Invoice lines — exported from Shopify (Settings → Billing), with subscription, usage and one-off charges kept separate. A list price is not evidence of what was paid.
  3. Expected cost — the cheapest published plan that covers the profile, computed from prices with a check date. Where the vendor sets the price in a calculator, the cell says "not published".
  4. Variance — invoice minus expected, per line and as a total.
  5. Rule — the billing rule that explains each gap: overage, minimum spend, seat count, VAT, annual discount.
  6. Verdict — keep, right-size, consolidate, or verify.
  7. Saving — monthly and annualised, with the assumption that has to hold.
  8. Risk — what changing it costs: data, theme code, integrations, features.

What the example found

  • Confirmed: $50/mo ($600/yr) on Gorgias. Basic costs $60 + (400 − 300) × $0.40 = $100. The same 400 tickets on Starter is $10 + 350 × $0.40 = $150, and Pro is $360. Basic stays the cheapest plan below about 1,050 tickets, so the saving does not evaporate at the next month's volume.
  • Candidate: $29/mo ($348/yr) by consolidating review apps. At 1,200 orders Judge.me is $15 flat and Fera is $29; keeping one drops the other's fee. This is a candidate, not a finding: Fera includes AI review replies at every tier, so the $29 may be buying something Judge.me does not do. The feature test comes before the number.
  • Unverified: two lines stay open. Klaviyo's $59 at 5,000 profiles cannot be recomputed, because Klaviyo sets that price in its own calculator and publishes no table. Postscript's $62 usage sits above its $49 floor, but its per-message rates are not published in a form we can re-add. Both cells say "unknown" instead of a number we cannot defend.

Why the "cannot verify" cells stay in

  • A report that turns an unknown into a number is worse than no report. Guessing the Klaviyo figure would make the whole table unfalsifiable, including the two lines that are solid.
  • Vendors that price in calculators (Klaviyo, Yotpo) cannot be reconciled from published data at all. The honest output is a specific question to the vendor: which tier is this account on, and what is the next band?
  • This is the difference between this report and asking an AI assistant to guess the bill: the assistant can produce a plausible number for every line. A reconciliation marks its own guesses.
  • The confirmed saving is small on purpose. Most real bills do not contain a dramatic error; they contain a plan that was right a year ago and a second app that does the same job.
