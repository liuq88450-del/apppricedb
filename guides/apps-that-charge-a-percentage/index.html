Apps that charge a percentage of your revenue, not a flat fee

A $149 subscription is easy to budget. A subscription plus 1.5% plus $0.20 per transaction is not, because the bill grows every time you sell something.

Figures below come from the vendor pricing pages we read on the dates shown on each app page (latest check 2026-11-27). The arithmetic is ours.

The worked example: 1,000 orders, $60 average order

$1,100 of that $1,462 (75%) is Recharge's percentage and per-transaction fee. The three apps with predictable bills together cost $213.

How to tell whether you are exposed

  1. Read the fee line, not the plan price. If the pricing page mentions a percentage, a per-ticket charge, a per-message segment or a per-seat charge, the plan price is the smallest part.
  2. Multiply by your own numbers. Take your monthly order count and average order value, apply the published rate, and compare that number with the plan fee. If the metered part is bigger, you are not on a plan, you are on a meter.
  3. Check what resets the tier. Contacts, products, tickets and orders all move you up; none of them move you back down automatically when a busy season ends.
  4. Ask what happens at the boundary. A store at 5,001 orders can pay nearly double the rate of one at 5,000, for one extra order.

What we do not claim

  • We have not run these apps on a live store. Everything here is arithmetic on published prices.
  • Where a vendor does not publish a rate (Klaviyo's calculator, Recharge's Scale tier, Fera's band mapping), we say so instead of estimating.
  • Prices change. Each app page carries the date we last read the vendor's page.
